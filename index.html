01

Explorer of Worlds

Most people spend their whole life inside one reality tunnel.

Not because they chose it. Because leaving is expensive. The world you grow up in hands you a map: what's normal, what's smart, what's embarrassing. Staying on the map is free. Stepping off it costs comfort and status, so almost nobody steps off.

Here's the part that interests me: every world is sitting on knowledge the other worlds need. Monks know things about attention that founders would pay for. Sales veterans know things about trust that engineers never learn. And none of it crosses over on its own. It just sits there, stranded.

So I cross over on purpose. I enter a world, live inside it until I understand it on its own terms, then carry the valuable parts into the next one. Outsiders misunderstand every world they haven't lived in. I'd rather not be an outsider to any of them.

  • 20+ countries

    Lived with locals across 20+ countries, mostly couch surfing. Not hotels and highlights: kitchens, family dinners, other people's routines. The fastest way I know to see my own assumptions from the outside.

  • sf zen center

    Resident volunteer at the San Francisco Zen Center, training clear thinking through meditation. A world built entirely around attention, and it works.

  • the side door

    Moved to the US alone at 18, mid-pandemic. Paid my own way through community college online while working, then targeted a transfer into UVA on need-based aid. Roughly 17% acceptance. I found the side door, walked in, and later chose to walk out.

  • inside sales

    Helped a two-decade sales veteran build his startup from the inside. The world where trust is the product and everything else is detail.

  • manual work

    Ran a dog-walking business solo: sales, marketing, client trust, a brutal schedule, all mine. 4.9 stars. Physical work with nowhere to hide.

The range isn't drift. It's the method.

02

Applied AI

In 2024 I left UVA to get genuinely good at one thing: applying AI to real problems.

Not the research side. The applied side. I want to know these models the way a mechanic knows a specific engine: what it's great at, where it lies, what it sounds like right before it breaks, and how to build things around both.

That kind of knowledge only comes from reps, so I've been collecting reps.

  • built + launched

    Built and launched qualitative city reviews for digital nomads. Google Maps reviews, but for deciding where to live. When I needed to understand my users, I flew to Thailand and talked to them in person, where they actually were. The product didn't survive. The speed and the go-to-the-source instinct did.

  • ai video labs

    Worked with a YC founder generating high-quality POV footage for top AI video labs. The unglamorous ground floor of frontier AI. I learned what labs actually need from their data, and how exacting "high quality" really is.

  • automation + ops

    Owned tech and automation (HubSpot) and parts of operations at a sales-driven startup. Building the machines behind the machine.

How I work: give me autonomy plus one experienced person who'll ask me hard questions, and I move fast. I learn by doing the thing badly, quickly, then doing it right. Light mentorship goes a long way with me. I once picked up professional video editing (multi-cam, color, sound, motion graphics) for UVA's business school this exact way: one-on-one, on the job, fast.
