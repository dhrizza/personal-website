The game I'm playing

If you're simplifying, I'm a traveler. The higher-level game is experimenting with ways to bring these worlds back to other people.

I'm trying long-form podcasts, code (this site is one attempt), and maybe shorter video. The space is saturated, so I'm still looking for a better angle. That's why I don't make much content yet.

The thing I keep noticing: a lot of people seem to be in the wrong world for them. I'd like to help them find the right one. Worst case, it's entertaining.

History

Worlds I've been in. Newest first.

No traditional work history here, and most of my potential is still unactualized. I've recently worked out some principles that are helping me move faster. This is also only the good stuff: plenty of failures are either too boring to list or things I'm not comfortable putting on the internet.

  • Left the University of Virginia in 2024 to get good at applying AI in the real world.
  • Built and launched my own product: real reviews of cities for people picking where to live, like Google Maps reviews but for places to live. Built mostly solo with AI, and flew to Thailand to talk to users in person.
  • Worked with a Y Combinator founder making high-quality POV footage for top AI video labs. Shut it down when it wasn't what we wanted to build.
  • Lived in Bangalore for a while to save money and look for a co-founder, in one of the biggest tech hubs outside Silicon Valley.
  • Lived as a resident volunteer at the San Francisco Zen Center. Meditated daily, mostly to think more clearly.
  • Been to more than 20 countries, often for a while, usually couch surfing with locals.
  • Hitchhiked about 3,000 km across the Sahara, and rode an iron-ore freight train through the desert, partly to get over a fear of death.
  • Biked and hitchhiked through Egypt, camping by the pyramids (vlogs here).
  • Couchsurfed in Iraq, which the US government says not to visit at all. Did my own research first. Far more normal than the news makes out.
  • Transferred into the University of Virginia from community college on need-based aid I aimed for. About 17% get in.
  • Edited video for the UVA business school: multi-camera, color, sound, motion graphics.
  • Moved from India to the US alone at 18, during the pandemic. Set up residency, paid my own way, did community college online while working.
  • A sales veteran mentored me and I joined his startup at 18. Ran the tech and automation (HubSpot) and part of operations.
  • Built and ran an entrepreneur-in-residence program end to end, including interviewing the founders.
  • Ran my own dog-walking and house-sitting business. 4.9 rating on a full schedule, doing all the sales and marketing myself.
  • Odd jobs to get by: machine operator for a military parts supplier, Walmart shelves, Target electronics.
  • At 14, made money online buying and reselling in-game items, building it to over $1,400, and resold game consoles. First time I noticed the pattern: buy cheap in one place, sell where it's worth more.
  • Went to a strict, selective school in India that didn't fit how I learn (undiagnosed ADHD). Learned most from debates with smart friends, not from class.
