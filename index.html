The through-line

At 14, growing up in India, I noticed that items in an online game were mispriced. I flipped them for about $1,400. Small money, but the pattern was set early: spot the inefficiency, act before it's obvious.

At 18 I moved to the US alone, mid-pandemic. I established residency, worked while putting myself through community college online, and deliberately targeted a transfer into UVA on need-based aid. It worked. One of the top public universities in the country, roughly 17% acceptance, and I'd found the side door.

Then in 2024 I left. The degree was a fine bet on the old world. But AI was rewriting what a career even looks like, and I'd rather be early to the new one than well-credentialed for the previous one. Same move I made at 14, just with higher stakes.

Along the way I've traveled through 20+ countries, mostly couch surfing and living with locals. I'm used to being the outsider in the room, and I've gotten good at earning trust fast anyway.

Some things I've done

Built a product, then flew to my users

I built and launched qualitative city reviews for digital nomads. Think Google Maps reviews, but for deciding where to live. When I needed to understand my users, I flew to Thailand and talked to them where they actually were. The product didn't survive. The instincts did: ship fast, go to the source, and don't fall in love with your own idea.

Helped build a startup from the inside

A sales veteran with two decades of experience took me on and I helped build his startup, owning tech and automation (HubSpot) and parts of operations. I got a working education in how deals actually close, compressed into months instead of years.

Worked inside the AI supply chain

With a YC founder, I generated high-quality POV footage used by top AI video labs. Unglamorous, precise work that taught me what frontier labs actually need from their data, from the ground floor.

Ran a one-person business

I ran a dog-walking business solo: sales, marketing, client trust, and a brutal schedule, all mine. Held a 4.9-star rating the whole way. Nothing teaches accountability like being the entire company.

Cut video professionally, learned on the job

I was a video editor for UVA's business school. Multi-cam, color, sound, motion graphics. I learned it fast, one-on-one with an experienced editor, which is how I learn best: real stakes, light mentorship, high autonomy.

Lived at the San Francisco Zen Center

I spent time as a resident volunteer there, training clear thinking through meditation. It sounds unrelated to startups. It isn't. Staying calm and honest under pressure is a work skill.

How I work, and what I'm looking for

Give me autonomy plus one experienced person who'll ask me hard questions, and I move. I learn by doing the thing badly, quickly, then doing it right. I understand people better than I understand businesses, so I gravitate toward work where talking to users, closing loops, and earning trust is the actual job.

I'm looking for early-stage teams: seed to Series A startups (especially AI-native and consumer), nonprofits, and solo operators. Small teams where a real human makes the hiring call by looking at the whole person, and where the work is a net positive. If that sounds like you, I'd genuinely love to talk.
